Subject: A free periodic-table activity for your chemistry class

Hi [Name],

I built Elementa, a free chemistry study site at [elementalearn.com](https://elementalearn.com/?utm_source=teacher_outreach&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=elementa_classroom).

Students can look up all 118 elements, compare properties, inspect simplified electron-shell and orbital diagrams, and practise with short quizzes. There's also a printable worksheet with an answer key. Students don't need an account; their study notes and progress stay in their browser.

One way to try it: ask students to compare sodium and magnesium, predict their outer-shell electron counts, then check the diagrams and explain how those counts relate to their groups. It should fit a short warm-up or revision activity.

Would this be useful in your class? I'd especially value feedback on any wording or diagrams that could leave students with the wrong mental model.
