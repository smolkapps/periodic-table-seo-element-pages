Teacher directions and answer key

Oxygen and sulfur

Compare electrons, shells, and periodic-table positions

Suggested use: Introductory chemistry, grades 9-12 (editorial suggestion).

Give students page 1. They need only a pencil and the printed data, with no website or account. Ask them to show electron totals before comparing shells. Review why an atom's total electron count differs from its outer-shell count.

Scope: These questions describe neutral atoms in their ground state. Shell counts are a simplified representation; use the model reminder to discuss what the rings leave out.

  1. Particle counts. Oxygen: 8 protons, 8 electrons. Sulfur: 16 protons, 16 electrons. Equal positive and negative charges make each atom neutral.
  2. Configurations. Oxygen: 2 + 2 + 4 = 8. Sulfur: 10 + 2 + 4 = 16. The noble-gas core must be included in the total.
  3. Shell counts. Oxygen: 2, 6. Sulfur: 2, 8, 6. Expanded sulfur configuration: 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p4.
  4. Periods. Oxygen is in period 2, with two occupied shells; sulfur is in period 3, with three occupied shells.
  5. Outer shells. Each has 6 outer-shell electrons. Oxygen has 2s2 + 2p4; sulfur has 3s2 + 3p4. Both are in group 16.
  6. Claim correction. Sulfur has 8 more electrons in total, while both outer shells contain 6. Sulfur has an additional occupied shell; its inner-shell counts are 2 and 8.
  7. Sketch correction. Use 2, 8, 6. The proposed 2, 6, 8 totals 16 but places the electrons in the wrong shells for ground-state sulfur. A correct total alone is not enough.
  8. Model limits. Accept comparisons of shell counts, occupied shells, or outer-shell counts. For a limit, accept that rings are not literal paths, or that the sketch does not show orbital shapes or electron probability distributions. Accept equivalent accurate wording.
